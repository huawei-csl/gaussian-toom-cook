# libgtc: design

This document describes how libgtc is put together: the algorithms and
how they call each other, how numbers are represented, how the algorithm
of each product is chosen, where the scratch comes from, and the
assembly. It is meant for readers of the source; the README says what
the library does and how to use it.

## Source layout

| path | contents |
|---|---|
| `include/gtc.h` | the public interface: configuration, contexts, rational and Gaussian products |
| `src/gtc.c` | the library as one translation unit: includes the parts below, then the public functions |
| `src/config.h` | which assembly exists for the target, the default thresholds and tables per microarchitecture |
| `src/ctx.c` | contexts, the scratch arena, the configuration and its resolution into per-size algorithm tables |
| `src/linear.c` | the linear-time helpers: signed values, additions, halvings, accumulation |
| `src/gauss.c` | the Gaussian base cases, `gtc_22` and `gtc_33` |
| `src/gauss_toom4.c` | `gtc_44` |
| `src/gauss_toom5.c` | `gtc_55`, and `gtc_q1`, the dispatch of the Gaussian products |
| `src/rational.c` | `r2gtc_22` .. `r2gtc_55`, their dispatch `r2gtc`, unbalanced products |
| `src/gtc_*_x86.S`, `src/gtc_*_arm.S` | the assembly, generated by `tools/gen_*.py` (`make asm`) |
| `src/libgtc.map` | the symbols the shared library exports: those of `gtc.h` |
| `tools/fetch_gmp.sh` | takes the declarations of the GMP internals used from the GMP source |
| `tools/tune.sh` | the per-size tables for a machine |
| `bench/` | `bench_gtc` (the comparisons with GMP), `bench_gauss` (the Gaussian products against three of GMP's) and `tune_gauss` (thresholds and tables) |
| `tests/` | `test_gtc` (black-box, against GMP) and `test_helpers` (white-box) |

The library is one translation unit so that the compiler sees every
call: the small per-node helpers are forced inline (`GTC_INLINE`), the
larger steps kept out of line (`GTC_OUTLINE`), and the dispatchers
`gtc_q1` and `r2gtc` are inline in each level that calls them, with the
cap as a constant. The order of the parts is the order of the functions
in the object, which affects code layout and was kept as measured.

## Numbers

Numbers are little-endian arrays of 64-bit limbs, as in GMP's `mpn`
layer; B = 2^64. A Gaussian product (ar + i ai)(br + i bi) with n-limb
components takes its factors in the closed first quadrant (ar, ai, br,
bi ≥ 0) and gives P + iQ, P = ar br − ai bi and Q = ar bi + ai br, as two
results of 2n+1 limbs in two's complement (P may be negative, Q is not).
A factor in another quadrant is rotated into the first by a power of i
(an exchange of the components and a sign), and the product rotated back
(`gtc_signed`): the signs are bookkeeping, never arithmetic.

Inside the levels, signed intermediate values are `sreg`s: a pointer to
a two's complement value of a fixed width and a lazy negation flag, the
value being (−1)^neg times the stored one. A signed linear combination
is one pass of addition or subtraction chosen by the flags; negation and
multiplication by i swap pointers or flip a flag. Halvings and the exact
divisions by 5 and 15 run as single passes, the latter by GMP's
`mpn_bdiv_dbm1c` step, or on some cores in the assembly by two carry
chains (see below).

## The algorithms

Each Gaussian level, `gtc_ii` for i = 2..5, splits its components into i
parts of k = ⌈n/i⌉ limbs (the last part h ≤ k limbs), evaluates both
factors at 2i − 1 points, multiplies the values pointwise (Gaussian
products of k or k+1 limbs, which recurse) and interpolates and
recomposes the 2i − 1 coefficients:

* `gtc_22`, Karatsuba: points 0, 1, ∞.
* `gtc_33`, paper I: points (0,1), (−1,1), (1,1), (i,1), (1,0). The
  value at i of a Gaussian polynomial is a Gaussian number whose two
  parts give two equations, so there is no division by 3.
* `gtc_44`: points 0, ±1, ±i, 2, ∞, with the evaluation of paper I and
  the interpolation of paper II; one exact division by 15.
* `gtc_55`, paper II: points 0, ±1, ±i, 1+i, 1−i, −1+i, ∞; divisions by
  2, 4, 8 and 5 only. The values at 1+i, 1−i and −1+i come from those
  at 1, i and −i.

Each rational level, `r2gtc_ii`, evaluates its factors at rational and
Gaussian points and makes three rational products and i − 2 Gaussian
ones (`r2gtc_22`: one of each). A Gaussian point gives two equations, its
real and imaginary parts, for the price of one Gaussian product:

* `r2gtc_22`, paper I: with a = a0 + a1 X and b = b0 + b1 X, the
  Gaussian product (a0 + i a1)(b0 + i b1) = c0 + i c1 and c2 = a1 b1 give
  ab = (c0 + c2) + c1 X + c2 X². The factors are the halves themselves:
  no evaluation.
* `r2gtc_33`, `r2gtc_44`, `r2gtc_55`, paper II: the rational points (0,1),
  (−1,1), (1,0), and the Gaussian points (i,1); (i,1), (−1−i,1); and
  (i,1), (1+i,1), (−1+i,1).

Where the papers evaluate at (1,1), libgtc evaluates at (−1,1), and
mirrors the other points by x → −x: the value of a factor at (−1,1) has
a nonzero top limb far less often than at (1,1), and so its product is
mostly one of k limbs rather than k+1. Where a top limb is nonzero, a
product of k+1 limbs is either taken as such or as the k-limb product
plus corrections for the top limbs, whichever is cheaper at that size
(`r2gtc_top_rule`, `gtc_q1_top`).

The evaluations, products and interpolation of a level share one block
of scratch laid out so that each product goes over evaluations that are
no longer needed, and so that the coefficients are made in their final
places in the result where possible (`gtc_44_layout`, `gtc_55_layout`,
`r2gtc_44_layout` and so on).

## Choosing the algorithm: caps, thresholds, tables

`gtc_ii`'s products are `gtc_jj` (j ≤ i) or a Gaussian base case, and
`r2gtc_ii`'s rational products are `r2gtc_jj` (j ≤ i), GMP's Karatsuba
or GMP's textbook product, its Gaussian products those of `gtc_ii`: a
product never recurses into an algorithm of a higher degree, as GMP's
`mpn_toomii_mul` never does. That bound is the cap i, a constant argument
of every dispatcher. `gtc` and `r2gtc` are the cap 5.

Within a cap, the algorithm of an n-limb product comes from the
thresholds of `gtc_config` (the largest algorithm allowed whose threshold
n reaches) or, below 1024 limbs, from a per-cap table that overrides them
by size: a Toom-4 level can then hand some sizes to Toom-3 and others to
Toom-4, which a pair of thresholds cannot say. A context resolves its
configuration once, when it is made, into `alg[cap][n]` and
`ralg[cap][n]` for n < 1024 (`resolve_gtc_table`,
`resolve_r2gtc_table`); above that the thresholds are evaluated directly.
The Gaussian base cases and the Karatsuba levels in assembly are
`GTC_ALG_LEAF` entries, called through `leaf[cap][n]`.

The defaults (`src/config.h`) were tuned per machine: the thresholds by
`tune_gauss`, the tables by `tools/tune.sh`, each kept only where whole
products got faster.

## Scratch

A context owns an arena used as a stack: each level takes its block
(`tmp_alloc`) and gives it back (`tmp_free`) in reverse order. A block
that does not fit comes from `malloc`, and the peak is recorded; between
top-level calls the arena grows to the peak (`arena_fit`), so that from
the second product of a size on nothing is allocated. Blocks start on a
cache line. Building with `-DGTC_NO_ARENA` takes every block from
`malloc`, so that the sanitizers and valgrind see each block's bounds.

## The assembly

Which kernels exist is decided at compile time (`src/config.h`): on
x86-64 ELF with BMI2 and ADX, and on aarch64 ELF, unless `GTC_NO_ASM` is
defined. Each `.S` file assembles to nothing on the other architecture.
The kernels are generated by Python scripts (`tools/gen_*.py`), whose
docstrings say what each kernel computes and how; the generated files
are checked in so that a build needs no Python.

* **The Gaussian textbook product** (`gtc_gmul_tbN`, 2 to 20 limbs). On
  x86-64 each component is a sum of two products, P = ar br + (~ai) bi +
  bi − bi B^n and Q = ar bi + ai br, so it needs additions only: rows of
  `mulx` whose low halves go along the CF chain (`adcx`) and high halves
  along the OF chain (`adox`), into a window of registers. From 12 limbs
  the vectors are cut into column blocks. On aarch64 there is one carry
  flag and a limb product is two instructions anyway (`mul`, `umulh`),
  and every size fits one window.
* **The Gaussian Karatsuba over {1, i}** (`gtc_gmul_kmN`, aarch64, 2 to
  10 limbs): three real products in Winograd's form, the linear work
  inside the rows of the scans, where the multiplier would otherwise wait.
* **The Gaussian Karatsuba levels** (`gtc_gkaraN`, 4 to 64 limbs): one
  `gtc_22` level each, calling the products of its halves through the
  context's leaf table. Each kernel has an entry `name_ns` that saves no
  callee-saved register, which only the levels call.
* **The linear passes** (`gtc_linear_*.S`, `gtc_avx512_x86.S`): the passes
  of the levels above, two carry chains at once where the level has two
  components to update (`adcx` and `adox` on x86-64; on aarch64 one flag,
  parked in a register between blocks of limbs): additions of both
  components, three-operand sums, halvings and shifts fused with the sum
  before them, the exact divisions by 5 and 15 fused with the sums that
  feed them, and whole steps of `gtc_55`'s interpolation in one pass
  each (`gtc_tc5*_n`): for instance a sum, its shift, the division by 5
  and the additions of the quotient into two coefficients. `r2gtc_55`'s
  interpolation runs on the same kernels. Some kernels divide by two
  carry chains of one cycle a limb, where GMP's exact division
  (`mpn_bdiv_dbm1c`) is one chain of two, a subtraction and a
  subtraction with borrow on its result. That division gives the
  quotient's negative: the code after takes −q with its flag flipped
  (`gtc_div15n_lsh_n`, `gtc_div5n_rlsh_n`, and `gtc_tc5q8_n` where q is
  added), or the numerator is negated first (`gtc_tc5s_n`'s (c − t)/5
  on the Intel cores).

On x86-64 the sections are aligned to 64 bytes, so that a change in the
size of the C code does not move the kernels within the 64-byte fetch
windows; such moves alone made whole products 0.1–0.2% slower or faster.

## Per-microarchitecture code

The microarchitecture is known at compile time (`-march=native`):
`__znver*` (AMD Zen, `GTC_ZEN`), `__AVX512F__` (Intel with AVX-512),
`__aarch64__`, or any other x86-64. Some code paths have two forms, each
faster on some machines; the macro that selects one (for instance
`GTC_TC5_PPIORI` or `GTC_RTC4_SHLD`) is defined next to the code it
selects, with a comment saying what each form does. The assembly makes
the same distinction with `#if` on the same macros, where a kernel is
written differently for the Zen (`shrd` is several micro-ops there) and
for the Intel cores. The default thresholds and tables in `src/config.h`
follow the same split.

To tune for another machine: `tune_gauss` for the thresholds, then
`tools/tune.sh` for the tables, then `bench_gtc` for whole products
against the defaults, keeping only what is faster.

## GMP

libgtc links against an installed libgmp. It calls the public `mpn` and
`mpz` functions, and a few that libgmp exports but only GMP's private
`gmp-impl.h` declares: `mpn_mul_basecase`, `mpn_toom22_mul` (its
rational base cases), `mpn_bdiv_dbm1c` (the exact divisions),
`mpn_addlsh1_n`, `mpn_sublsh1_n`, `mpn_rsblsh1_n`, `mpn_addlsh2_n`,
`mpn_rsblsh2_n`, `mpn_rsh1add_n` and `mpn_rsh1sub_n` (the latter two in
the portable build only), and for the benchmarks `mpn_toom33_mul`,
`mpn_toom44_mul` and `mpn_toom6h_mul`. The `lsh` and `rsh1` functions
exist only in a libgmp built with its assembly; libgtc takes
`mpn_rsh1add_n` and `mpn_rsh1sub_n` as weak symbols and falls back to
two passes without them, but needs the others.
`tools/fetch_gmp.sh` copies their declarations verbatim from the GMP
6.3.0 source into `build/gmp_decls.h`.

## Tests

`test_gtc` checks every public function against GMP's `mpn_mul_n`,
`mpn_mul`, `mpz_mul` and an `mpz` Gaussian reference: every size up to a
few hundred limbs over adversarial input patterns (all ones, runs, sparse
limbs, extreme top limbs), random sizes up to 100,000 limbs, fixed and
random configurations (thresholds down to the minimum, so that the
algorithms interleave at tiny sizes, and random per-cap tables), every
algorithm at the top and below every other, and the result area and the
scratch guarded by canaries. `test_helpers` includes the library source
and checks the internal helpers and every assembly kernel against `mpz`
or GMP in all their modes, including branches that the current
interpolation sequences never take. `make check` runs them with and
without the assembly, with the portable halving, and on x86-64, whatever
the machine, also with the Zen's code paths and with the other cores',
C and assembly, the latter's Gaussian Toom-5 also at small sizes.
